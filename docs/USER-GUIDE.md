# Akeno ColorVision — User Guide

This guide explains everything in plain language. You don't need any technical knowledge.

---

## 1. What the app does

Some colors look the same to you even though they are different — for example many reds, greens,
browns and grays. Akeno ColorVision changes the colors on your **whole screen** so those colors become
**different colors or different brightness** that you can tell apart.

* It works everywhere: the desktop, websites, YouTube, Discord, pictures and games like Minecraft.
* It does **not** cure color blindness. Some colors will look unusual — that's on purpose. What matters is
  whether things that used to look identical now look different.
* It works offline and never records your screen.

---

## 2. Starting the app

1. Open the folder where you unzipped Akeno ColorVision.
2. Double-click **AkenoColorVision.exe** (the icon with the eye).
3. The first time, Windows may say *"Windows protected your PC"*. Click **More info**, then **Run anyway**.
   This happens with new apps that aren't signed by a big company.

The app has a menu on the left:

| Page | What it's for |
|---|---|
| **Home** | Turn the filter on/off, choose a profile, change the strength. |
| **Color Filters** | See and adjust what each profile does. |
| **Calibration** | Find the best settings for your eyes (about 5 minutes). |
| **Gaming** | Settings for Minecraft and other games. |
| **Color Identifier** | Find out the name of any color on screen. |
| **Profiles** | Rename, copy, delete, import or export profiles. |
| **Settings** | Shortcuts, start with Windows, theme, privacy. |
| **About** | Check the filter works, diagnostics, compatibility. |

The box at the bottom left always shows whether the filter is **ON** or **OFF**, and has the
**Reset colors** button.

---

## 3. Turning the filter on and off

* Click **Turn filter on** on Home (or in the bottom-left box).
* Or press **Ctrl + Alt + Shift + F** anywhere — even in a game.
* A small message "Filter ON" / "Filter OFF" appears at the top of the screen (you can switch that off).

**Strength:** on Home, move the **Correction strength** slider. Left = closer to normal colors, right = stronger.

**Compare:** on the Color Filters page, press and hold **Hold to compare with original colors** to see the
normal colors for a moment.

**Closing the window** does not stop the filter — the app keeps running in the **system tray** (the small
icons next to the clock; click the **^** arrow if you don't see it). Right-click the eye icon there for a
menu, or choose **Exit** to quit completely.

---

## 4. Choosing a profile

A *profile* is a set of color settings. On Home, click one of the cards:

| Profile | Good for |
|---|---|
| **Red-Green Assistance** | Everyday use. Reds get a little purple/pink, greens a little yellow, and reds become a bit lighter. |
| **Protan Assistance** | If reds tend to look dark, muddy or almost black. |
| **Deutan Assistance** | If reds and greens both look brownish or yellowish. |
| **Strong Separation** | When you really need to tell things apart (games, maps, charts). Looks unnatural. |
| **Brightness & Contrast** | Only makes colors more vivid and contrasty. Helps when colors look dull, but doesn't separate red from green by itself. |
| **Minecraft — Nether Visibility** | Dark reds, browns and grays like in Minecraft's Nether. Brighter and strongly separated. |
| **Your own profiles** | Made by Calibration or by customizing a copy. |

Not sure? Start with **Red-Green Assistance**, or do the Calibration.

## 5. Calibration (recommended)

Calibration finds the settings that work best for **your** eyes on **this** screen.

Before you start: if *Night light* is on (Windows Settings → System → Display), turn it off for now.
The app pauses the filter during calibration so you see the original colors.

1. **Your experience** — pick the sentence that fits you best. It's only a starting point.
2. **Color pairs** — you'll see two big squares. Click how different they look:
   *Clearly different*, *Slightly different*, *Almost the same* or *Exactly the same*.
   Just answer honestly — there are no wrong answers. Some pairs are easy on purpose.
3. **Choose what works best** — you'll see the same colors corrected in different ways (A, B, C, D).
   Click **Choose option…** under the one where the squares in each pair look the **most different**.
   Don't worry if it looks strange. Then you choose the strength, then a final fine-tune.
   **Try on whole screen (10 s)** shows an option on your entire screen for 10 seconds.
4. **Game-style scene** — a test picture with dark reds, browns and grays, like the Nether. Compare the
   original with your new profile and with the Minecraft preset. You can **load your own screenshot**
   (it stays on your computer). Choose which one to use for Minecraft.
5. **Save** — give your profile a name and choose whether to turn the filter on now.

The app never tells you which type of color blindness you have — it isn't a medical test. It only
looks for settings that help. You can calibrate again any time.

## 6. Minecraft

Go to **Gaming**.

* **Minecraft profile:** what's used while Minecraft is on screen. *Minecraft — Nether Visibility* is a good start;
  calibration may suggest your own profile instead.
* **Switch to the Minecraft profile when Minecraft is in front:** on by default. When you go back to the
  desktop or close the game, your normal profile comes back after a moment.
* **Also turn the filter on while playing:** turn this on if you want the filter in the game even when
  it's normally off.

**If the colors don't change in the game:** the game is probably in *exclusive fullscreen*. In Minecraft
Java Edition press **F11** to switch to a normal window, then maximize it. In other games choose
*Borderless* or *Windowed* in the video settings.

**Tip for the Nether:** also turn up Minecraft's own *Brightness* (Options → Video Settings).

Other games: start the game, go to **Gaming → Add another game or app → Show running apps**, pick the
game and click **Add selected app**.

## 7. What color is that? (Color Identifier)

* Point the mouse at a color anywhere on the screen and press **Ctrl + Alt + Shift + C**.
* A box appears next to the mouse with the **color name** in words, its **category**
  (Red, Green, Brown, Gray…) and its code (for example *#8B2424*).
* **Live identifier** (on the Color Identifier page) follows the mouse and keeps naming the color under it.
  Press the shortcut again to stop.
* **Identify in 3 seconds** gives you time to move the mouse to the right place.

With the filter on, the app tells you whether it's showing the **original** color or an **estimate** of it.
The first time, it runs a short check (a small colored square flashes for a moment).

---

## 8. If something looks wrong — Emergency reset

If the screen colors ever look wrong or too extreme, any of these will bring back normal colors immediately:

1. Press **Ctrl + Alt + Shift + Backspace**.
2. Click **Reset colors** (bottom left in the app, or in the tray menu).
3. Double-click **AkenoColorVision-EmergencyReset.exe** in the app folder. This even works if the app is frozen.

After a reset or after the app closed unexpectedly, the app starts in **safe mode**: the filter stays off
until you turn it on. To force safe mode, **hold Shift** while starting the app.

Restarting your computer also always returns the screen to normal.

---

## 9. Settings

* **Start Akeno ColorVision when I sign in to Windows** — so it's always ready.
* **Start hidden in the system tray**.
* **Turn the filter on when the app starts**.
* **Keyboard shortcuts** — click a box and press the new keys. If a shortcut is already used by another
  program, the app tells you and keeps the old one.
* **Dark / Light theme**.
* **Keep a local log file** — helps fixing problems. Nothing is sent anywhere.
* **Export diagnostics** — creates a text file you can send to whoever helps you. It contains no screenshots
  and no personal information.

---

## 10. Privacy

* No account, no internet connection needed, no ads, no tracking.
* The app never records or uploads your screen.
* Your profiles and calibration answers are stored only on your computer in
  `%LOCALAPPDATA%\AkenoColorVision`.

---

## 11. Good to know

* **One filter for all monitors.** If you have two screens, both get the same correction.
* **Screenshots** usually show the original colors, not the filtered ones.
* **HDR:** if HDR is on, colors can look different. The app shows a note; try with HDR off if things look odd.
* **Windows' own color filters** (Settings → Accessibility → Color filters) add on top of this app. Use one or the other.
